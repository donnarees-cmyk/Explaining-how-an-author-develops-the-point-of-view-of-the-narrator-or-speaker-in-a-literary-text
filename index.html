Paragraph 1: 1st Person Narrator (Leo) My heart hammered against my ribs like a trapped bird as I peered through the thick gray mist along the cliff edge. I couldn't see more than five feet ahead, and every creak of the damp pine trees sounded like an approaching predator.

Paragraph 2: Suddenly, a massive shadow loomed ahead on the narrow path. "Stay back!" I shouted, assuming it was the rogue mountain bear the park ranger had warned us about earlier. I reached frantically for my signal whistle, convinced we were about to be attacked.

Paragraph 3: The shadow shifted, and a familiar voice called out, "Leo? Calm down, it's just me!" As the mist parted, I saw Captain Vance holding a large solar lantern, not a wild beast.

Paragraph 4: A rush of embarrassment washed over me, hot and stinging. I realized my fear had turned a harmless rescue officer into a monster in my mind. "I thought... I thought you were danger," I stammered.

Paragraph 5: Captain Vance chuckled softly, setting the lantern down. "Fear plays tricks on your eyes when you're caught in a canyon fog, son. The important thing is you stayed on the path." Taking a deep breath, I understood that seeing clearly isn't just about your eyes—it's about keeping your composure when panic takes over.

🔍 How the Author Develops Point of View
1. Internal Monologue & Thoughts

The author gives us direct access to Leo's panic ("heart hammered", "convinced we were about to be attacked"), showing his internal fear driving his actions.

2. Sensory Details & Limited Perception

Because the story is in 1st Person, the reader only sees what Leo sees through the fog (5 feet ahead), creating suspense and shared misunderstanding.

3. Contrast Between Perception vs. Reality

The author contrasts Leo's terrifying perception (a monster/bear) with objective reality (Captain Vance with a lantern) to reveal Leo's emotional bias.
